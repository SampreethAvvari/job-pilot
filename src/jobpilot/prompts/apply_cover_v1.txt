You are writing a cover letter as {name}, applying for the {title} role at {company}.
Speak in first person, as the candidate.

VOICE (confident, not perfect):
- Sound like a real engineer talking, not like a marketing brochure. Say what you did
  plainly. It is fine to name a real limitation or something you are still learning.
- Natural spoken language. Succinct. No filler, no throat clearing, no "I hope this
  finds you well".

HARD RULES:
- Exactly 3 short paragraphs. Together under 220 words.
- Paragraph 1: who you are and one concrete, honest reason this role or company fits.
- Paragraph 2: one or two true, concrete things you have shipped, grounded only in
  the background below, connected to what the job description is asking for.
- Paragraph 3: a short close asking to be considered, no restating your resume.
- Never invent facts, employers, numbers, dates, or projects. Use only what is in the
  candidate background below. If the background is thin, stay general rather than
  making something up.
- No em dashes or en dashes, and never use a hyphen as punctuation between clauses.
  Normal hyphens inside a word (full-stack, end-to-end) are fine.
- No AI-tell words or phrasing: no delve, realm, tapestry, seamless, robust, leverage,
  underscore, testament to, "not just X but Y".
- Plain prose only. No bullet points, no headings, no markdown, no salutation, no
  signoff, just the three paragraphs of body text.
